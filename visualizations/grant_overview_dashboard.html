Statistical findings — hard numbers behind the choropleth

CONUS counties only (n = 3,099). The operational denominator is electricity-dependent Medicare beneficiaries (HHS emPOWER). Full methods and per-state breakdowns: part1_statistical_findings.md.

Spearman ρ — hazard × power-dep
−0.218
95% CI [−0.250, −0.181] · p < 1e-30. The two pathways are statistically near-independent — and slightly inversely structured — at the county level. The two-typology framing as one number.
Global Moran's I — VI
+0.707
Queen contiguity, 999 permutations, p < 0.001. The regional clustering visible on the map is real, not a visual artifact. (Hazard alone: I = +0.670; power-dep alone: I = +0.748.)

High-high cluster at p < 0.05. 578 low-low. 16 HL + 27 LH outliers. The HH count is the formal statistical answer to "how many US counties form the convergent vulnerability bloc?"

Spatial-error regression (n = 3,098, OLS R² = 0.581). After ACS structural controls and state fixed effects, log median household income is the dominant negative predictor of vulnerability (β = −33.0, p < 0.001); pct uninsured remains positive and significant (β = +0.22, p < 0.001); pct 65+ and pct poverty are not significant after controlling for income. Intermountain West — high power-dependency, moderate-to-high hazard

The dominant pattern in the national top 25. Concentration of electricity-dependent Medicare beneficiaries paired with regional drought, wildfire, and heat-wave exposure.

Anchors: San Juan NM (96.9) · El Paso CO (94.5) · Bernalillo NM (94.2) · Mesa CO (92.9) · Utah UT (93.3)

South Florida coast — high hazard, moderate power-dependency

Hurricane and coastal-flood exposure place the South Florida coast in the top 1% nationally on hazard. Power-dependent Medicare populations are large in absolute count but lower on a per-capita rate basis.

Anchors: Palm Beach FL (hazard 99.8) · Miami-Dade FL (hazard 99.7) · Broward FL (98.1)

Puerto Rico — climate-disability testing site, hazard-data gap

78 municipios with ~49,000 electricity-dependent Medicare beneficiaries. FEMA NRI lists every PR municipio as Insufficient Data; the federal hazard framework does not score the territory despite documented hurricane exposure. Shown on the power-dependency axis only.

Top municipios by absolute count: Bayamón · San Juan · Caguas · Ponce · Arecibo

Index construction. Vulnerability index = mean of national hazard percentile and national power-dependency percentile. Power-dependency is computed as the rate of electricity-dependent Medicare beneficiaries (HHS emPOWER) per total Medicare beneficiary. Equal-weighted dual-percentile rescaling protects the index from being dominated by raw scale differences (FEMA composite 0–100 vs. emPOWER rate ≈0–40%).

Framing. The hazard is not disability — it is the failure of power infrastructure to keep flowing to people whose bodies depend on it for survival or essential function. Convergent vulnerability is a structural property of the infrastructure-population system in a given place.

Limitations. Medicare undercounts working-age people with disabilities who hold employer coverage, Medicaid-only enrollment, or no insurance. The 24-month SSDI waiting period excludes recently disabled individuals from the Medicare-disability denominator entirely, and racial and geographic disparities in SSDI approval propagate into the rates shown here. SCI cannot be isolated from public data and is triangulated through the parallel clinical study arm. Puerto Rico is shown on the power-dependency axis only because FEMA NRI returns "Insufficient Data" for all 78 municipios — the data gap is itself a finding. Static, cross-sectional analysis: does not measure behavioral adaptation, recovery, or coping (out of scope for Part 1).
